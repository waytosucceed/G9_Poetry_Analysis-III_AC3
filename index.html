Nothing Gold Can Stay
By Robert Frost

Nature’s first green is gold,
Her hardest hue to hold.
Her early leaf’s a flower;
But only so an hour.
Then leaf subsides to leaf.
So Eden sank to grief,
So dawn goes down to day.
Nothing gold can stay.

Hints:
1. Subject:
Beautiful things that don't last.
(The title tells us this straight away: "Nothing Gold Can Stay.")
2. Most prominent poetic devices:

  • Metaphor — "Nature's first green is gold" directly equates the newest leaves with gold, without "like" or "as," establishing value and rarity from the first line.
  • Allusion — "So Eden sank to grief" references the biblical Garden of Eden's fall from paradise, borrowing an entire narrative of lost perfection in five words.
  • Symbolism:
    • Gold — a conventional symbol, universally understood to represent value, purity, and rarity, used here for the leaf's first colour.
    • Eden — a conventional symbol drawn from religious tradition, representing an original, unspoiled state of perfection.
    • Dawn — a conventional symbol for beginnings and freshness, paired here with "day" to represent the ordinary state that inevitably follows.

3. Theme(s):
The poem gives us three examples:

  • A leaf loses its gold colour
  • Eden was lost
  • Dawn always turns into day.

Putting these together, the theme is:
Nothing beautiful or perfect can stay that way forever — everything precious eventually changes or fades.
